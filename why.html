What it actually does

A website isn't a brochure. It's your hardest-working employee.

One that works nights and weekends, never calls in sick, and answers the same three questions a hundred times a day so you don't have to.

01 / Found

It gets you found on Google

When someone searches your trade plus your town, you want to be the result they tap, not the competitor two doors down.

02 / Trusted

It makes you look legit

People judge your whole business by how your site looks in the first half-second. A clean site says "these folks are the real deal" before you say a word.

03 / Open 24/7

It answers when you can't

Hours, menu, prices, directions, booking. The stuff people call to ask is right there, at 11pm, while you're asleep.

04 / Yours

You actually own it

Unlike a social page you rent from an algorithm, your site is yours. It doesn't vanish when a platform changes the rules.

05 / Converts

It turns a search into a customer

A tap to call, a booking button, directions to your door. It closes the gap between "found you" and "walked in."

06 / Level

It levels the playing field

A sharp one-page site can make a two-person shop look every bit as sharp as the chain down the road. Often sharper.

"But..."

The four things people say to talk themselves out of it.

"I already have a Facebook / Instagram."
Good, keep it. But you don't own it, and 84% of people trust an actual website more than a social page alone. Social is where people find you. A site is where they decide to trust you and where Google actually ranks you.
"My business runs on word of mouth."
And the first thing that new referral does is look you up. Word of mouth gets your name in their head; your website is what they see when they check you out. No site, and the trail goes cold.
"I'm too small to need one."
Small is exactly who this helps most. A simple site is often the cheapest customer you'll ever buy, and it makes a small shop look established and safe to try.
"Websites are expensive."
Agencies charge thousands, sure. I don't. A clean one-page site is $600 flat, and the calculator below shows how fast a handful of extra customers pays that back. It's usually weeks, not months.
